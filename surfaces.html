Partial derivatives

Each partial is the slope of a trace: slice the surface with x = x₀ or y = y₀ and you get an ordinary curve.

Tangent plane

Near the point, the surface and this plane are almost the same. That is what being differentiable means.

A surface made of curves

A bicubic Bézier patch uses a 4 × 4 grid of control points. Fix u and you get an ordinary Bézier curve in v, and the other way round.

Matrix form

Only the heights change here, so the z-coordinates fit in one 4 × 4 matrix Z. The selected control point is highlighted.

Curve at u = u₀

Mix each column of Z with the weights bi(u₀). The result is the control polygon of the yellow curve.

Setting up the integral

The same solid, written in different coordinates. The highlighted factor is the Jacobian, which says how much volume one small cell takes up.

Adding up the cells

Cut each variable's range into n pieces and add f · (cell volume) over the n³ cells. The cells in the picture are those pieces.
